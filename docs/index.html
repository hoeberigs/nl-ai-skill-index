§ 04Scarcity

Which roles employers cannot fill

Because the same boards were captured every day for six months, each advert can be followed from the day it appears to the day it goes. Roles whose adverts run longest are the ones the market struggles to close.

Roles are discovered from the adverts themselves rather than taken from a fixed list: every vacancy is embedded with a multilingual model and clustered, so the groupings reflect how Dutch employers actually write about work.

§ 07Method

How this was measured, and where it can mislead

Two corrections do most of the work. Both are the difference between a number that looks reasonable and one that is right.

The scraper sees about half of what exists

Which changes how long adverts really run

Full method and known limits

Pipeline

  1. An upstream tracker scrapes Dutch job boards daily and commits its database, giving this analysis a versioned, reproducible input.
  2. Rows are one per advert per scrape, collapsed to a vacancy identity on normalised title and employer, merging recruiter repost chains.
  3. Detection probability and its day-to-day persistence are measured on days a vacancy was provably live; the grace period is sized from the persistence parameter.
  4. Survival is Kaplan-Meier with log-rank tests. Left-censored spells dropped, right-censored retained.
  5. Vacancies embedded with a multilingual MiniLM encoder, reduced by PCA, clustered with k-means, k by silhouette.
  6. Skill trends use Cochran-Armitage across months with Wilson intervals per point.

Where it can mislead

  • An advert disappearing is not a hire. Roles leave a board when filled, withdrawn, expired or defunded. This measures time advertised, never time to hire.
  • Skills are read from advert text, retrieved for of vacancies. Every skill figure uses that subset as its denominator.
  • Two boards are not the market. Roles filled through networks or internal moves never appear.
  • Clusters summarise a continuum. Silhouette near 0.15 means adjacent roles shade into one another rather than forming discrete types.
